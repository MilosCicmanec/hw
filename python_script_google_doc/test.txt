How reliably can an isolated [rewrite] How reliably can an isolated knower construct and justify knowledge using only the internal logic of a formal system?  Stefan Zweig's Schachnovelle explores how the protagonist operates inside a closed axiomatic system. He is foricbly//forcibly isolated by the Gestapo and in order to not lose his mind he studies and memorises [pause] 150 master chess games. This process establishes the closed axiomatic system in his mind. Once he has studied the games enough he is ready to start pruducing//producing knowledge on his own. The core problem here is the crisis of justification. The protagonist shows that an isolated knower can generate vast amounts of knowledge based only on deduction. This is shown by him generating thousands of new chess matches purely in his imagination. His mind starts to work like a recursive algorithm that explores complex chess positions. The critical problem arises when he attempts to justify this knowledge. In mathematics and formal logic, justification relies heavily on peer reviews of community of knowers to ensure objective truth. Without a physical opponent, he is forced to split his conciousness, acting as both the creator(White pieces) of knowledge and the valiator//validator(Black pieces). Lacking the objective feedback Dr. B's mind starts to spin in an infinite and destructive feedback loop which the author labels as chess poisoning. This demonstrates that while isolated axiomatic systems can generate vast amounts of knowledge that is internally valid, they also rely on external peer reviews for verification.
