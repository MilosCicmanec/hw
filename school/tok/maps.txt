In what ways do values affect the production of knowledge?

To the modern eyes, the map Hereford Mappa Mundi may seem as an impossible fantasy rather than a real functional map. This map demostrates very well that what we recognize as valid knowledge depends heavily on our culture and personal beliefs. It was created in 1300 AD and this reflects heavily on its structure. Geometricaly it's incorrect and this is because the autor valued Christian theology more than the true proportions. Jerusalem occupies the exact geometrical center and is portraid as the absolute center of the world. East is oriented at the top towards the garden of eden rather than north. Physical terrain coexists directly with historical monuments that represent biblical events such as parting of the red sea and the tower of babel. The autor also spreads his antisemitic beliefs through depicting jews that worship the golden calf. The African population is depicted as mythical beings maybe even monsters instead of humans. These details provide compeling evidence that production of knowledge can never be culturaly neutral. The creator of this map was not failing at modern empirical navigation but rather prioritized his culture's theological beliefs. He organized spatial and historical reality into a moral and spiritual narrative that was supported by his beliefs.
